Flow Metrics · Charlie’s Epic Flow Clinic

Welcome to Flow Metrics

A flow clinic for your delivery teams: give it your work items — a completed date, a start date and a type — and it works out throughput, cycle and lead time, work in progress, and the age of everything still open, grouped into Flow, Delivery and Health, for one team or across all of them.

Everything you enter stays in this browser. Nothing is uploaded — there is no account and no server.

Start Fresh opens Your Data, where you paste a report out of Jira or add work items one at a time — a board kept by hand works just as well.

Load Sample Data fills every chart with three invented teams and nine months of work items, each team named for the one thing it shows, so you can see what the app does before typing anything of your own. The figures are made up.

Press an item to edit it, or add one by hand. A row pasted from an export and a row typed in here are the same thing once they are saved — both go through the same checks, and both are yours to correct.

Clean Up Old Data

Work items pile up, and years of them make every paste and every backup heavier without telling you anything new. This drops the ones older than a cutoff you pick. You choose how far back to keep, and see exactly what would go, before anything is deleted.

Teams, ARTs & Stages

Teams

Each team keeps its own work items. The picker in the header chooses which one the dashboard is showing; Settings are shared by all of them.

Project id is the letters at the front of the team's issue keys — DAE for DAE-1552. It is what lets one export covering several teams be pasted in one go and split between them; it changes no figure. Leave it blank and the team simply takes nothing from a split.

WIP ≤ is how much work this team means to have open at once, and 85% ≤ how long it means an item to take — the promise the 85th percentile is read against. Both are drawn as a line on the charts and neither changes a figure: they are what the figures are compared to. Leave either blank and the app states the number without judging it, which is what it does today.

85% ≤ is about work that has FINISHED — it is checked against the items this team delivered. The separate ageing threshold in Settings is about work that has not: how long something can sit open before it is flagged. They are different questions, so they are usually different numbers — and the threshold is normally the lower of the two, because an item that has already passed your target is too late to rescue. A promise of 10 days is often watched with a threshold around 6.

Agile Release Trains

An Agile Release Train groups teams together. It is a label only — every figure is still worked out per team — and All Teams gains a picker for it. Teams need not be on one.

Workflow Stages

A stage is a part of your workflow you want to measure the wait in — Build, Review, Test. Paste a Time in Status export with a column per status and each item's days land in the stage you have matched that status to. Several statuses can feed one stage: list them all and their days are added together. No export? Every stage you add here also gets a box on the work item form, so the days can simply be typed in.

You type the status names, and this app never stores them. A status name is work-system text, so it is matched while your paste is being read and then thrown away with the rest of it — what is saved is the stage name you chose and a number of days. That is also why there is no button to pick the statuses up out of an export: it would put those words into storage, which is the one thing this app will not do. Match on the status name exactly as Jira spells it; capitals and punctuation don't matter.

Settings

Everything the charts depend on is here, and nothing else — a setting that changes nothing is worse than a missing one. Every one of them is shared by every team; the two figures a team sets for ITSELF are on its own row under Teams & Stages.

Labels & Work Types

The words on the charts, and what counts as a defect.

Unfinished work only — nothing that has completed is ever counted here. Not the same as a team’s 85% ≤ target, which is checked against work that has finished; set this lower than that target so something is flagged while there is still time to act on it.

Cycle time, lead time and the ageing threshold skip weekends — an item started on a Friday and finished on the Monday takes one day, not three. Public holidays are not known to this app and still count as working days, wherever you are.

Anything whose Type is blank, or doesn't match the defect text above, counts as ordinary planned work.
Work Type Filter List

The choices in the Work Type filter on the dashboard. Display is what you pick; Value is the text matched against your Type column. Leave the All row alone — it means "no filter".

DisplayValue Actions

Paste Several Teams at Once

One export covering more than one team, split by the project id at the front of each issue key — DAE-1552 goes to whichever team is set to DAE. It is read once, as one export, so every team is read the same way. Nothing is written until you press one of the load buttons, and a team this paste does not name is never touched.

Back Up & Restore

One JSON file holding every team, their work items and your shared settings — a copy you keep, independent of this browser. It is the only copy of your data that exists anywhere else, so it is worth taking one.

Restoring replaces every team in this browser. You'll see what the file holds and be asked to confirm first. A backup file is also how you move your data to another device — download it here, and restore it there.

Start Again

Deletes every team and every work item in this browser. Your settings and your theme are kept.

Your Link

If your paste had an issue key column, those keys travel in the link — they are what names the items on the charts, and a link without them would show a different picture from yours. Nothing else out of your work system does: no summaries, no statuses, no names.

This is a snapshot, frozen as it looks right now — later edits won't appear, so send a fresh link when the numbers move. There's no way to withdraw one once sent, so treat it like emailing a spreadsheet. If you trimmed the history, whoever opens it is told which date the copy starts from.
